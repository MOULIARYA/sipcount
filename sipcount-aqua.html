Behind the Cloud

The "Cloud" is on the Ground

Generative AI feels like magic. You type a prompt, and a server in the cloud instantly writes code, drafts essays, or renders artwork. But the cloud isn't in the sky—it's massive, hyper-heated physical infrastructure sitting right here on earth.

The Hidden Cost of Computing

Every AI query requires intense computational power. To keep these hyperscale server racks from melting, data centers evaporate millions of liters of freshwater daily through cooling towers. Meanwhile, the regional power grids feeding them consume even more water to generate that electricity.

We are trading our most vital physical resource for digital convenience. As global economies push for aggressive GDP growth, balancing this massive industrial resource drain against municipal water stress is a math problem that simply doesn't add up.

Screen Time for Generative AI

You wouldn't use your phone without checking your screen time or battery level. Sipcount applies that same visibility to your AI usage. We serve as your native Screen Time for Generative AI, stripping away the abstraction of server telemetry and translating your estimated water footprint into tangible, everyday metrics—down to the milliliter.

We are building a movement of digital sobriety. By seeing the actual cost of your input queries and output generations, you can write smarter prompts, choose lighter models for simple tasks, and hold tech infrastructure accountable.

How the numbers work

Servers get hot when they answer you, and they're cooled with water that evaporates. The power stations making their electricity use water too. Sipcount adds both up for every prompt.

  • Longer questions and longer answers use more. Answers count more than questions, because writing text out is slower, hotter work than reading it in.
  • "Reasoning" models think for longer, so they use about ten times the water. Making an image is like sending about ten text prompts.
  • Where the data centre is matters: cooling technology and the local power mix change the total.

All figures are estimates from published research. Constants .
