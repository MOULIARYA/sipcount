Sipcount — Privacy

Last updated 30 September 2026

Sipcount counts. It measures how long your prompts and the replies are, and nothing about what they say. Nothing you type is stored, and nothing leaves your device.

What Sipcount looks at

To estimate the water used to run a prompt, Sipcount needs its size. On the pages it supports — chatgpt.com, claude.ai and gemini.google.com — it counts the number of characters you sent and the number the reply came back with, notices which script they are written in so the count is right for Hindi as well as English, reads the model name shown on screen, and times how long the model took.

The text itself is measured and immediately discarded. It is never copied, never stored and never transmitted — there is no code in Sipcount that could do so.

All of it lives in your own browser. You can see exactly what is there at any time — the popup has a Copy CSV button that hands you everything Sipcount holds about you, one row per day — and Clear data removes it.

Nothing is sent anywhere

The browser extension declares no network permission at all. You can check this yourself: open chrome://extensions, click Details on Sipcount, and see that it asks only for storage. An extension without network permission cannot contact a server, so there is no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting and no account.

Calibration mode

Switched off unless you turn it on. It records one row per prompt — the same counts and timings described above, still no text — so the estimates can be replaced with measurements. It stays on your device until you export it and choose to send it to us. Turning it off stops the recording.

Children

Sipcount is designed to be used by people of any age, which is why it keeps nothing about anyone. It asks your age once, uses it only to decide how the app behaves, and stores the answer as a setting rather than a number. Sharing is switched off under 13. Syncing between devices, when it arrives, will be available from 18 — see below.

Syncing between your devices

Not switched on in this version. When it is, it will work from a code generated on your device and never sent to us. Your daily totals would travel between your own devices encrypted with a key derived from that code, so we could not read them even in principle. Your age, your prompts and your email address would never be part of it, and the date would remain the finest unit of time recorded.

If any of this changes

This page is versioned in the open, alongside the code, at github.com/mouliarya/sipcount. If Sipcount ever begins collecting something, it will say so here first and in the app before it does.
